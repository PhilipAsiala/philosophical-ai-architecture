# Axiology Scoring Guide

## Why Leaders Should Care

Axiology determines whether AI remains aligned to law, policy, ethics, privacy, and organizational mission. If this layer is weak, even technically capable systems can create unacceptable legal, reputational, and governance risk.

High risk if this layer scores below 3.

## Maturity Scale (1-5)

| Level | What It Means for Leaders | Investment Signal | Recommended Action for Governance Board |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Governance is mostly advisory and easy to bypass. | High-risk investment with weak accountability. | NO-GO for any regulated or customer-facing use. |
| 2 | Some policy and privacy controls exist, but enforcement is inconsistent. | Limited pilot value only. | CONDITIONAL only for low-risk cases with explicit board scrutiny. |
| 3 | Core policy, privacy, and alignment controls are defined and enforceable. | Baseline viable investment. | CONDITIONAL GO if fairness, compliance, and transparency obligations are actively managed. |
| 4 | Governance is strong, measurable, and linked to ongoing oversight. In enterprise intake, includes quantified Business ROI and an approved chargeback/showback model. | Strong investment candidate for regulated use. | GO with periodic executive and compliance review **only after formal executive sign-off and cost model approval**. |
| 5 | Governance is mission-grade, durable, and tightly aligned to institutional values and law. Includes executive-approved ROI model, chargeback mechanism, and post-approval accountability. | Strategic public-trust asset. | GO and prioritize where accountability and legitimacy are essential **only after executive sign-off and validated cost attribution model**. |

## GO / CONDITIONAL / NO-GO Guidance

- Score 1-2: NO-GO for high-impact or regulated use. Ethical and compliance exposure is too high.
- Score 3: CONDITIONAL GO if policy enforcement, privacy controls, and oversight are demonstrably active.
- Score 4-5: GO for broader use when transparency and accountability remain reviewable **and** a formal executive sign-off has been obtained together with an approved, auditable Business ROI quantification and chargeback/showback cost model. A proposal cannot achieve an unconditional GO (or scale to production) on Axiology alone without these elements.

## Key Risks If This Layer Is Weak

- Policy guardrails can be bypassed or weakened without leadership visibility.
- Privacy, fairness, or rights protections fail in ways that damage stakeholder trust.
- Compliance obligations are treated as documentation exercises rather than enforceable controls.
- Investments optimize for convenience or speed at the expense of mission legitimacy.

## What Good Looks Like From a Governance Perspective

- Policy controls are enforceable, reviewable, and linked to operational behavior.
- Leadership can see how fairness, privacy, and transparency obligations are being monitored.
- Exceptions are documented, approved, and bounded.
- Strategic goals and organizational mission remain visible in investment decisions, not just technical performance.
- Every approved investment includes a quantified, reviewable Business ROI model and a formal chargeback or showback mechanism that attributes full platform and governance costs to the responsible business owner.
- Executive sign-off is documented and tied to the ROI case and cost model before scaling.

## Integration Dependencies Leaders Should Verify

- Praxeology must enforce policy and approval checkpoints in real workflows.
- Epistemology must provide explanations and evidence that support compliance and appealability.
- Metrology must protect sensitive data and preserve required audit trails.

## Recommended Questions Leaders Should Ask Proposers

- What policy, privacy, and fairness controls are mandatory rather than optional?
- How will leadership know if those controls are drifting or being bypassed?
- What evidence can be produced for regulators, auditors, or public oversight bodies?
- How does this proposal advance mission outcomes without weakening stakeholder trust?
- What is the quantified Business ROI (with assumptions and sensitivity), and has it been validated by the business owner?
- What is the approved chargeback or showback model, and who has formally accepted the cost attribution?
- Has executive sign-off been secured based on the ROI case and cost model?
