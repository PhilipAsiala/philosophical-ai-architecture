# Epistemology Scoring Guide

## Why Leaders Should Care

Epistemology determines whether leaders can trust the system's conclusions. If this layer is weak, the organization may act on plausible-sounding but unverified outputs, creating mission failure, compliance exposure, and reputational risk.

High risk if this layer scores below 3.

## Maturity Scale (1-5)

| Level | What It Means for Leaders | Investment Signal | Recommended Action for Governance Board |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Outputs are largely unverified and difficult to defend. | High-risk investment. | NO-GO for any consequential decision support or automation. |
| 2 | Some checks exist, but evidence and reliability remain inconsistent. | Limited experimental value only. | CONDITIONAL only for low-risk pilots with strong supervision. |
| 3 | Core validation and provenance controls exist. | Baseline viable investment. | CONDITIONAL GO for bounded use cases if leadership accepts residual risk and oversight. |
| 4 | Reliability, traceability, and consistency are strong. | Strong candidate for mission use. | GO with regular performance and compliance review. |
| 5 | Conclusions are audit-grade, evidence-backed, and operationally defensible. | Strategic trust asset. | GO and prioritize for high-value, high-accountability use cases. |

## GO / CONDITIONAL / NO-GO Guidance

- Score 1-2: NO-GO for decisions affecting customers, compliance, enforcement, or stakeholder trust.
- Score 3: CONDITIONAL GO where outputs are reviewed and validated before action.
- Score 4-5: GO for wider use when supported by appropriate governance and human oversight.

## Key Risks If This Layer Is Weak

- Leaders receive conclusions that cannot be justified with evidence.
- AI systems amplify hallucinations or unsupported claims.
- Audit and appeals processes break down because rationale is missing or inconsistent.
- Confidence is overstated, leading to poor funding or operational decisions.

## What Good Looks Like From a Governance Perspective

- Leadership can ask why a conclusion was reached and receive a clear, source-backed answer.
- Uncertainty is visible and treated as a governance input, not hidden from decision-makers.
- Evidence chains are preserved for review, audit, and challenge.
- Reliability is measured continuously rather than assumed.

## Integration Dependencies Leaders Should Verify

- Ontology must provide stable semantic context and relationships.
- Praxeology should not act on outputs unless epistemic controls are adequate.
- Axiology depends on traceable explanations and defensible reasoning for compliance review.

## Recommended Questions Leaders Should Ask Proposers

- What evidence supports the system's outputs, and how is that evidence shown?
- How does the system distinguish between a likely guess and a verified conclusion?
- What happens when the model is uncertain, inconsistent, or unsupported by source material?
- Can the organization defend these outputs in an audit, appeal, or oversight hearing?
